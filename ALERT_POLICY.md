# Motion-first object detection

The app samples the existing per-camera motion detector. No motion region means no object inference. For an eligible region it runs YOLO11n on a padded crop, maps object boxes to the original frame, and checks whether a person or vehicle overlaps the motion region. It confirms motion either from coherent adjacent-frame optical flow or repeated, spatially matched observations; vehicle tracks additionally require position change on the repeated-observation path. Only a qualifying crop causes full-frame inference on the same sampled frame. Crop and full-frame detections are combined as scene context.

Only qualifying moving crop objects can start an object clip. Once the gate passes, the final notification rules evaluate the full scene: two or more people at any time, or one person from 21:00 inclusive until 05:00 exclusive in Africa/Johannesburg. Vehicle-only detections and a single person during the day do not notify. The person count is from one sampled frame, with overlapping duplicate boxes suppressed; model errors can still overcount or miss people. The capture time is retained with the sampled frame. A 20-second per-camera, per-scenario cooldown groups repeated detections. Motion and object clips remain independently recorded. Qualifying WhatsApp alerts are queued when the short object clip closes, rather than waiting for a potentially much longer motion clip; the existing WhatsApp setting still applies. Motion-clip metadata includes checks and scene labels; a label alone does not imply an alert was sent. The app no longer starts its separate vehicle-verifier worker or uses the weak full-frame model in this path.

To reduce false person alerts, crop-only person boxes shorter than 6% of the frame height need a matching person in the full-frame pass. A two-person notification requires at least two full-frame boxes verified using YOLO11s on padded crops; verified boxes are deduplicated so one person cannot count twice. The verifier runs in the same inference worker only for potential groups and checks at most four candidates. When its weights at `models/yolo11s.pt` are missing or verification fails, group notifications are suppressed and the detector reports degraded status. The overnight single-person rule can still use a supported crop detection.

These checks add inference time and can miss real people, especially distant, partially obscured or briefly visible people. A full-frame false positive can still be verified incorrectly. A small development replay rejected the bird/pole false alert and a coat false positive while confirming two saved two-person examples. It also detected the second person later in one example, and one labelled two-person frame was missed. The sample is too small to establish reliability across cameras, night scenes or live Pi scheduling.

Motion and object inference is asynchronous and bounded to the latest pending frame per camera. Samples may be dropped under load. Cropping the largest region, occlusion, camera shake, flow threshold, detector confidence and tracked-box jitter can produce misses or false positives. If a crop is not recognized, full-frame detection does not run, even if the frame contains an object. In offline tests the known partly obscured camera 801 passage was missed by this strict gate. The saved camera 701 bus clips produced no qualifying moving objects, and two camera 701 passing vehicles qualified; these few examples are not a representative accuracy study. Validate live performance, temperature and alerts before relying on the system.
